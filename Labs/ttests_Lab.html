Study Overview

Researchers want to investigate whether going on walks with babies impacts their vocalisation patterns. They have two objectives:

  1. Test whether there is a difference between parent-initiated and infant-initiated speech in parents who walk with their babies.

  2. Investigate whether time spent vocalising differs depending on whether the parent is carrying their infant in a backpack or pushing them in a buggy.

The researchers recruited 36 participants and split them into a backpack group and a buggy group. They then sent participants on a 15-minute walk with their infant and recorded the amount of time infants spent vocalising over the course of the walk. They also recorded the time the infant spent vocalising when the parent initiated the speech, and the time the infant spent vocalising when the infant initiated the speech.

Checking Assumptions

Assumptions should always be checked before test results are evaluated. If assumptions are violated, it could indicate that the statistical results are inaccurate.

The assumptions of an independent-samples \(t\) test are:

  1. The dependent variable must be normally distributed within each group

  2. Observations must be independent of each other

  3. The two groups must exhibit approximately equal variance (homogeneity) in the dependent variable

The assumptions of a paired-samples \(t\)-test are:

  1. The difference between the paired variables must be normally distributed.

  2. Participants must be independent of each other

  3. Values must be paired in some way

Inferential Analysis

An \(t\)-test produces a statistic that quantifies the degree of difference between two means. Recall that you’re testing the following two hypotheses:

Objective One:

\[H_1: \mu_{infantInitiated-parentInitiated}\neq 0\] Objective Two:

\[H_1: Vocalisation_{buggy} \neq Vocalisation_{backpack}\]

There are many ways you could write this up, but you should at least include the following information:

  • alpha level
  • number of participants (n)
  • participant demographics (mean age, age sd, and age range of sample; gender distribution of sample)
  • results from assumptions checks
  • descriptive data for each group (M and SD)
  • the type of test used (independent or paired-samples?)
  • independent and dependent variables tested
  • \(t\)-statistics
  • degrees of freedom
  • p-values
  • confidence intervals
  • effect sizes

The proper style of reporting the results from a \(t\)-test looks like this:

\(t\)(##) = #.##, \(p\) = .###, 95% CI = [#.##, #.##], \(d\) = #.##.

Note that the numbers inside the curly brackets refer to the degrees of freedom. Please refer to the lecture slides for a specific example.
